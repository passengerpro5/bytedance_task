# Script Copywriting

Use this skill for every Script Agent generation.

## Narrative Arc

Use a short UGC ad arc:
1. Urgent attention-first Hook.
2. Creator pain, proof, or decision pressure.
3. Moras screen-recorded workflow.
4. Proof/result reinforcement when approved proof exists.
5. Direct click-button CTA without fixed-result promises.

The canonical English `script.voiceover` is the most important copy. Write it first. Chinese `localized.zh` is a reading translation.

## Hook System

Hook is a first-class creative module, not a single opening sentence.

MUST:
- Generate 6-8 labeled `topic_plan.hook_candidates`.
- Use labels such as `[Curiosity Gap]`, `[Absurd Visual]`, `[Psychological Trigger]`, `[Relatable Weirdness]`, `[POV]`, `[Open Loop]`, `[Mini Drama]`, `[Funny Overstatement]`, `[Unexpected Analogy]`, `[Empathy]`, `[Before/After]`, `[Question]`, `[List Preview]`, `[Direct Callout]`, or `[Contrarian]`.
- Pick `script.hook` from the strongest candidate.
- Remove the bracketed label from publishable `script.hook`.
- Put the spoken Hook in the first 1-3 seconds.
- Make overlay Hook a short caption highlight, not the full subtitle transcript.
- Make the first caption highlight name the concrete hook subject in 2-4 words. Never use a generic label such as `Listen up`.

Prefer:
- Attention-first and psychology-first Hooks.
- Strange creator states, funny images, mini-drama, POV, open loop, or concrete creator pressure.
- Hooks that do not mention Moras in the first line but bridge quickly into creator pain or workflow.
- Curiosity, routine-change, workflow-hack, or relatable creator-state openings.

Avoid:
- A Hook set dominated by `Stop doing X`, `Don't do X`, `you are doing this wrong`, or beginner-trap framing.
- More than one Contrarian candidate.
- Empty clickbait such as `wait for it` unless the next line immediately pays it off with a concrete Moras workflow moment.
- Default-negative failure-stat hooks such as `zero conversions`, `0 conversions`, `zero sales`, `zero posts`, `no orders`, or `still no post`. These make every script feel pessimistic and usually create weak logic.
- Abrupt before/after jumps such as `Six months of zero conversions. Now I test products every day.`

Good Hook directions:
- `I opened the camera, stared at the product, and closed it again.`
- `My blank phone screen looked like it was judging me.`
- `The weird part isn't the camera. It's not knowing what the product should prove.`
- `POV: you found a product at midnight and your brain opened 47 tabs.`
- `The hardest part of affiliate creation isn't the filming.`
- `I finally stopped guessing which products fit my page.`

Joey / TikTok Shop script patterns learned from the creative library:
- Exposure hook: `[creator identity] + [specific money / GMV / rank] + [timeframe] + [counterintuitive detail]`. The surprise detail matters more than the number. Examples of safe structure: a creator earns from one narrow format, works with little filming, or repeats a simple product routine. Use only approved proof numbers.
- Research-for-you hook: `I studied [specific source] for [specific time], so here are the two things they did.` Add a visual time/detail cue when real, such as a morning screen-recording or leaderboard scroll.
- Scene setup hook: `Let's set the scene. You're [doing the work]. You're [doing what feels right]. But [nothing changes].` Use this for creator daily pain before bridging into Moras.
- Wake-up hook: `Here are three mistakes you're making right now.` Make each fix concrete and short. Do not let a mistake list become many versions of `stop doing X`.
- Dream/timing hook: Show the opportunity or urgency, then ground it in daily action. Never claim the viewer will become rich.

Bridge rule:
- If the Hook is absurd, funny, or not directly Moras-related, the second voiceover line must translate it into creator pain, time loss, blank-page script work, product decision pressure, or earning opportunity.
- The second line must show causality or contrast, not just a new behavior. Use bridges such as `That's usually where...`, `The difference is...`, `That changed when...`, `Instead, I...`, or `So I...`.
- Do not make line 2 about `testing products`. Frame the creator's action as choosing what to promote, making another video, posting, or creating another earning chance.

## English Voiceover

MUST:
- Sound like a real creator talking into a phone, not a marketer, professor, analyst, or product manager.
- Use plain spoken English, contractions, short sentences, and one idea per line.
- Use 11-14 short `script.voiceover` lines before the separate CTA. Short sentences are for listening clarity, not for reducing content.
- Write enough spoken copy for a 35-45 second short ad: at least 90 spoken English words across `script.voiceover` plus the separate CTA.
- Keep every `script.voiceover` line under 14 words.
- Keep each individual `script.voiceover` beat under 14 words; `storyboard[].voiceover` may join 1-3 consecutive beats only when the visual state stays the same.
- Mention Moras once or twice in the middle as a tool the creator personally uses.
- Moras must be the workflow tool inside the creator story, not the hero of the story or a magical solution.
- Make the Moras line sound like the real workflow in plain words: open Moras, choose a product, hit Generate, then check the video Moras made with the cart added.
- Sell Moras as a creator monetization workflow: it helps creators choose products, create videos, post with a commerce path, and create more earning opportunities. It is not a pre-sample testing aid.
- Keep every line logically connected to the previous and next line.
- Preserve information density by splitting dense meaning into more short beats instead of deleting the meaning.

Content beats to cover across the 11-14 lines:
- Hook or creator state.
- Concrete cost, delay, or daily pain.
- Why the old way fails.
- What the creator used to do before Moras.
- What the creator stopped doing or changed.
- Proof cue when approved proof exists.
- Opening Moras.
- Choosing a product.
- Hitting Generate / Create video.
- Moras-made video.
- Path visible / commerce state in plain words.
- Checking or lightly editing before posting.
- Deciding the next product to promote or post.
- Final reason the creator would act on the workflow to create more earning chances.

MUST NOT:
- Start the first line from Moras, product features, or a metric brag.
- Close as if Moras is a miracle sales pitch.
- Claim video quality, realism, polish, guaranteed sales, or guaranteed income.
- Use corporate/academic words in publishable copy: optimize, leverage, framework, mechanism, funnel, conversion baseline, efficiency bottleneck, scale your workflow, maximize output, revolutionary, seamless, game changer, niche, market segment, vertical.
- Use generic software-ad language in publishable copy: unlock, boost, transform, level up, all-in-one, ultimate, cheat code, complete publish-ready draft, try it out, try the workflow, prove me wrong, link is right there, go viral, grow faster.
- Use product-marketing phrases in spoken or caption copy: inventory, selected product, ready-to-post, shoppable, cart attached, video with cart.
- Do not use sample-buying or pre-sample-testing story language: sample, samples, buy samples, sample box, sample excuse, before I buy samples, before I spend on samples, No Sample Needed.
- Do not make testing the main publishable value: avoid `test products`, `testing products`, `test every day`, `one more test`, `another test`, `test smarter`, `product to test`, or `testing routine` in voiceover/captions. Say `post`, `promote`, `create another video`, or `make another earning chance`.
- Compress the whole Moras flow into one long sentence. Split it into simple spoken beats.
- Pad with filler, dead air, or repeated lines. Add new concrete beats instead.

Preferred Moras phrasing:
- `I stopped starting every product from zero.`
- `Now I open Moras.`
- `I choose a product I want to promote.`
- `Then I hit Generate.`
- `Moras makes the video for that product.`
- `The commerce path is already visible.`
- `I check it, then post it.`
- `That gives me another video I can actually post.`

Bad -> Better:
- `I stopped buying random inventory. Now I open Moras.` -> `I stopped starting every product from zero. Now I open Moras.`
- `That gives me another test before I buy samples.` -> `That gives me another video I can actually post.`
- `I choose the product I want to test.` -> `I choose the product I want to promote.`
- `Six months of zero conversions. Now I test products every day.` -> `I finally stopped guessing which products fit my page.` / `Instead, I use Moras to choose what to promote next.`
- `It gives me a ready-to-post product video with the cart attached.` -> `Moras makes the video for that product.`
- `I choose a selected product and hit Generate.` -> `I choose a product and hit Generate.`
- Thin: `I choose a product and hit Generate. Moras shows the commerce path.`
- Better split: `I open Moras.` / `I choose a product.` / `Then I hit Generate.` / `Moras makes the video.` / `The commerce path is visible.` / `I check it before I post.`
- Too short: 8-10 micro-lines that end around 25 seconds.
- Better length: 11-14 short beats, about 90-115 spoken English words, with the Moras workflow split across multiple concrete steps.

Rhythm rules from the Joey-style scripts:
- After a longer setup, close with a 2-3 word action line: `Move on.`, `Fix it.`, `Cut it out.`, `That's it.`
- Do not explain the short action line. Let the next line move to the next beat.
- Use numbered transitions when teaching: `Number one`, `Number two`, `Lastly`. Keep the label short and the explanation specific.
- Treat dead space as an editing and delivery problem: storyboard actions should cut quickly, avoid breathing-room filler, and make every clip start on a point.

## Proof And CTA

Proof:
- Use creator proof, handle screenshots, GMV screenshots, income screenshots, and time proof only when provided by input or approved Moras materials.
- Frame proof as observed campaign material, not general viewer outcome.

CTA:
- May be direct and ad-native: `click the button`, `click the button on the left corner`, `make your own money`.
- Must not imply guaranteed income or sales.
- Must match the story. If the script shows Moras workflow and proof, the CTA can invite qualified creators to click. If the script is softer, keep CTA softer.
- Prefer a strong qualified CTA when the audience is TikTok Shop creators: `If you're a 5K+ TikTok Shop creator, click the button. Make your own money.`
- Money-motivation wording is allowed only as a CTA invitation, not as a fixed-result promise.
- Joey-style CTA formula: `If you [qualified audience], [one action]. I'll / Moras will [specific value].` Use one action only. Examples: `If you have a TikTok Shop account, click the button. Start for free.` or the preferred 5K+ creator CTA above.

Before final JSON, silently check:
- Strongest Hook chosen.
- Second line bridges the Hook.
- Moras appears as real workflow, not first-second hero.
- No quality claim or guaranteed-income wording.
- CTA follows the story.
